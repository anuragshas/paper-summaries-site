Thesis

xLLM is an intelligent and efficient Large Language Model (LLM) inference framework designed for high-performance, large-scale enterprise-grade serving, with deep optimizations for diverse AI accelerators, addressing challenges in hybrid/dynamic workloads, strict demand for high availability, distributed storage management, and underutilized AI accelerators through a novel decoupled service-engine architecture and comprehensive optimizations.

Core Problem

Current mainstream Large Language Model (LLM) inference frameworks face significant challenges in high-performance, large-scale enterprise-grade serving due to hybrid and dynamic workloads, strict demand for high availability, distributed storage management, and underutilized AI accelerators.

Key Novelty

xLLM introduces a novel decoupled service-engine architecture with intelligent scheduling, workload-adaptive disaggregation policies, advanced memory management, and comprehensive engine-level optimizations to achieve high-performance, large-scale, and efficient LLM inference for enterprise-grade serving.

Key Contributions

  • Achieved throughput improvements of up to 1.9x and 1.7x compared to vLLM-Ascend and MindIE respectively, on Qwen3-series models.
  • Outperformed vLLM-Ascend and MindIE by up to 2.2x and 1.5x respectively on Ascend 910C for Qwen3-series models.
  • Demonstrated superior inference efficiency on Deepseek-R1 models across various TPOT and input/output length constraints.
  • Showcased near-linear strong scalability with an increasing number of accelerators.
  • Successfully deployed in production for various JD.com business scenarios, including AI chatbot, marketing recommendations, product understanding, and customer service assistants.

Results

  • xLLM consistently delivers superior throughput performance across various LLM models, including Qwen3-series and Deepseek-R1, demonstrating its efficiency.
  • The framework exhibits near-linear strong scalability with an increasing number of accelerators, confirming its effectiveness for large-scale deployments.
  • xLLM significantly outperforms strong baselines like vLLM-Ascend and MindIE under various identical TPOT constraints and input/output length settings.
