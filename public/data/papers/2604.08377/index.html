SkillClaw: Let Skills Evolve Collectively with Agentic Evolver

Authors: Ziyu Ma, Shidong Yang, Yuxiang Ji, Xucong Wang, Yong Wang, Yiming Hu, Tongwen Huang, Xiangxiang Chu

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2604.08377

Generated summary artifact for 2604.08377

Thesis

SkillClaw introduces a framework for collective skill evolution in multi-user agent ecosystems by transforming ordinary interaction trajectories into shared evidence, enabling an agentic evolver to autonomously update skills through refinement and creation, thereby allowing knowledge to accumulate and propagate across users over time in a continuous evolution loop.

Core Problem

Large language model (LLM) agents that rely on reusable skills for complex tasks often have static skill sets, leading to repeated rediscovery of solutions and failure modes across different users and over time, preventing the system from improving with experience.

Key Novelty

Collective skill evolution in multi-user agent ecosystems, driven by an agentic evolver and a closed-loop validation process.

Key Contributions

  • SkillClaw achieves consistent performance gains across diverse real-world agent tasks on WildClawBench, demonstrating the effectiveness of collective skill evolution.
  • Significant relative gains observed over 6 days: +11.72% for Social Interaction, +52.00% for Search & Retrieval, +88.41% for Creative Synthesis, and +33.33% for Safety & Alignment.
  • Controlled validation showed an average performance gain of +42.1% on tasks specifically designed to isolate common failure modes and resolve procedural knowledge deficiencies.
  • The system enables monotonic improvement, where only validated skill updates demonstrating better performance are accepted, preventing skill pool degradation over time.

Results

  • SkillClaw consistently improved performance across all four evaluated categories on WildClawBench over 6 days, demonstrating a stable evolution pattern.
  • The system effectively resolves primary bottlenecks and progressively consolidates locally effective updates into a stable skill set.
  • Social Interaction showed early and sharp improvements, Search & Retrieval exhibited a staged improvement trajectory, Creative Synthesis had a large early jump, and Safety & Alignment improved reliability under real-world constraints.
  • Controlled validation experiments confirmed that skill evolution is particularly effective when failures arise from missing or incorrect procedural knowledge, showing an average gain of +42.1%.

Limitations

  • The study represents a small-scale test of collective skill evolution with limited user queries, feedback signals, and interaction depth.
  • Future work is needed to explore scaling up the number of users, extending the time horizon, and introducing more diverse tasks and validation conditions to further enrich the evolution trajectory and system performance.
