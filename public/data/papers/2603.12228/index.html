Neural Thickets: Diverse Task Experts Are Dense Around Pretrained Weights

Authors: Yulu Gan, Phillip Isola

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2603.12228

Generated summary artifact for 2603.12228

Thesis

Pretrained models, especially large ones, possess a 'thicket' regime where the neighborhood around their weights is dense with diverse task-specific experts that can be discovered through simple random perturbations and ensembling, rather than requiring complex sequential optimization.

Core Problem

The traditional view treats a pretrained parameter vector as a starting point for iterative adaptation, often implying that finding good solutions requires structured, multi-step optimization methods.

Key Novelty

The discovery of 'neural thickets' - dense, diverse clusters of task-improving experts in the weight space neighborhood of large pretrained models, making simple random guessing and ensembling effective for post-training.

Key Contributions

  • RandOpt is O(1) in training time (wall-clock) due to its fully parallel nature, offering significant speedup over sequential baselines which are O(T).
  • Achieves state-of-the-art or competitive results on a wide range of tasks including mathematical reasoning, code generation, creative writing, chemistry, and visual question answering.
  • Demonstrates that sufficient pretraining and model scale are essential for the 'thicket' regime to emerge and for RandOpt to be effective.
  • Distillation can reduce RandOpt's inference-time cost (K forward passes) to a single model while retaining comparable performance, at a minor training cost (approx. 2%).

Results

  • Solution density of task-improving perturbations increases monotonically with model scale, showing that larger pretrained models inhabit 'thickets' of good solutions.
  • Local neighborhoods around pretrained weights are diverse, with sampled perturbations acting as specialists for different tasks (Spectral Discordance increases with model size).
  • RandOpt, a simple parallel random guessing and ensembling algorithm, achieves competitive or superior accuracy compared to complex sequential post-training methods (PPO, GRPO, ES) across various LLM and VLM tasks.
  • Ensembling (K>1) is crucial for RandOpt's performance, significantly boosting accuracy compared to single-perturbation selection (K=1).
  • The performance gains observed are partly due to reasoning improvements and partly due to superficial fixes like output formatting.

Limitations

  • RandOpt is not suitable for training large neural networks from scratch and struggles on small pretrained models due to low solution density.
  • The current approach may saturate, suggesting a need for more structured methods to find improvements beyond the local thickets.
  • Inference-time cost requires K forward passes, which can be high. While distillation can reduce this, it introduces tradeoffs (no longer fully parallel, additional training flops, specific to categorical reasoning).
  • Majority-vote ensembling is not directly applicable to structured prediction tasks (e.g., story generation, image generation) without modification.
  • The paper characterizes properties of the pretrained landscape but doesn't fully explain the mechanisms by which thickets emerge or what pretraining objective creates them.
