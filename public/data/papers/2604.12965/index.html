Efficient Retrieval Scaling with Hierarchical Indexing for Large Scale Recommendation

Authors: Dongqi Fu, Kaushik Rangadurai, Haiyu Lu, Yunchen Pu, Siyang Yuan, Minhui Huang, Yiqun Liu, Golnaz Ghasemiesfeh, Xingfeng He, Fangzhou Xu, Andrew Cui, Vidhoon Viswanathan, Lin Yang, Liang Wang, Jiyan Yang, Chonglin Sun

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2604.12965

Generated summary artifact for 2604.12965

Thesis

It is possible to efficiently scale large-scale foundation retrieval models for recommendation by jointly learning a hierarchical index (HILL) using cross-attention and residual quantization over the model's memory (MoNN), and by leveraging high-quality data from intermediate index nodes for 'test-time training' to improve inference performance.

Core Problem

Effectively and efficiently deploying large-scale foundation retrieval models, which leverage deep and complex neural networks, remains a critical challenge for recommendation tasks. Existing quick-win solutions like offline pre-computation or model distillation fail to fully utilize the models' representational and inference capabilities, primarily due to high inference and retrieval costs.

Key Novelty

A novel Hierarchical Index Learning (HILL) method that jointly learns a hierarchical index for large-scale foundation retrieval models (MoNN) to enable efficient, exactness-aware retrieval and generate high-quality data for test-time training.

Key Contributions

  • The MoNN Large model, combined with HILL, achieved a -1.70% reduction in Normalized Entropy (NE) and a +9.4% increase in Recall on internal Meta Ads data, demonstrating significant performance gains.
  • Stacked 2-layer MoNN architectures with HILL were shown to provide an effective-efficiency balance, reducing infrastructure costs while maintaining competitive performance.
  • On public benchmark datasets (Gowalla, Yelp2018, Amazon-Book), HILL (Ours) consistently outperformed strong baselines like NESCL across Recall@20 and NDCG@20 metrics.
  • Online A/B tests in Meta Ads production confirmed significant gains, with a 2-Layer MoNN (L1: MoNN Large, L2: MoNN Small) leading to a +2.57% improvement in the online ads metric.
  • Ablation studies verified the importance of all proposed training optimization tricks for HILL: Softmax Temperature Scheduler, Balanced Index Distribution, and Warmup Strategy.
  • The Expectation-Maximization (EM) approximation for HILL achieved competitive performance, offering a resource-efficient alternative to the full version.
  • Parameter analysis showed that a small amount of precisely selected test-time data, filtered by $\phi_{\text{DEP}}$ and $\phi_{\text{IR}}$, is sufficient to achieve leading performance.

Results

  • The proposed Hierarchical Index Learning (HILL) method, integrated with MoNN foundation models, consistently achieves significant improvements in retrieval performance (NE, Recall, NDCG) across both internal Meta Ads data and public benchmarks.
  • The work demonstrates an effective-efficiency balance for large-scale foundational retrieval models, enabling high performance while managing infrastructure costs through model stacking.
  • Online A/B tests at Meta confirm the practical utility and substantial gains of the proposed approach in a real-world production environment for advertisement recommendations.
  • Intermediate nodes in the learned hierarchical index provide a valuable source of high-quality data for 'test-time training,' which further boosts inference performance.
  • The study reveals that a small amount of precisely filtered test-time data is sufficient for achieving leading performance, indicating efficiency in data utilization.

Limitations

  • The paper suggests that future work could explore weighing new data pairs with weak signals and even negative signals for fine-tuning, implying that the current approach primarily focuses on positive strong signals.
  • Adding contrastive learning loss functions during the fine-tuning process is also mentioned as a promising future direction, suggesting it's not fully explored in the current method.
