Causal Foundation Models

Authors: Christopher Stith, Hossein Rahmani, Jesse C. Cresswell

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03003

Generated summary artifact for 2609.03003

Thesis

Causal Foundation Models (CFMs), which are pretrained neural networks leveraging in-context learning, provide a strong and efficient alternative to traditional causal inference methods by estimating causal quantities on new datasets without fine-tuning, demonstrating competitive performance with significantly faster inference.

Core Problem

Causal inference traditionally requires a laborious and repetitive workflow where a new, problem-specific pipeline must be constructed, involving model proposal, estimator selection, and training, for every new task, without effective knowledge transfer or reuse.

Key Novelty

The introduction of Causal Foundation Models (CFMs) that apply the foundation model paradigm of large-scale pretraining and in-context learning to causal inference tasks, enabling efficient, zero-shot causal effect estimation.

Key Contributions

  • Causal Foundation Models (CFMs) achieve competitive causal effect estimation performance on semi-synthetic RealCause-Lalonde benchmarks.
  • CFMs demonstrate 1-2 orders of magnitude faster inference times compared to traditional causal estimators, largely eliminating per-task training and tuning overhead.
  • CausalPFN, a specific CFM, exhibits particularly strong performance, often matching or exceeding traditional methods in accuracy while providing a substantial speedup in inference.

Results

  • Causal Foundation Models (CFMs), particularly CausalPFN, achieve competitive or superior performance on PEHE (Precision in Estimation of Heterogeneous Effects) and ATE Relative Error benchmarks compared to highly optimized traditional causal estimators like T-Learner and IPW.
  • CFMs offer a dramatic speedup in inference time (1-2 orders of magnitude faster) compared to traditional models, as they perform inference directly without per-task training or hyperparameter tuning.
  • The amortization of Bayesian inference via pretraining in CFMs effectively eliminates the computational overhead typically associated with hyperparameter tuning and cross-fitting loops in traditional causal analysis workflows.

Limitations

  • Existing CFMs are often specialized along specific axes (e.g., binary treatment, backdoor identification, particular causal quantities), and a single general CFM supporting multiple regimes and estimands is a future goal.
  • The quality of the synthetic training prior directly impacts CFM performance and generalization, highlighting the need for more quantitative measures of causal prior coverage and improved prior design.
  • Some existing CFMs exhibit prior-induced bias due to priors being supported on Data-Generating Processes (DGPs) with low confounding, necessitating methods like one-step posterior correction.
  • There are indications of low coverage in the credible intervals provided by CausalPFN, suggesting that uncertainty quantification needs further improvement.
  • Errors can occur in CFMs when a post-treatment covariate is passed to the model at inference time, indicating a need for better handling or filtering of such inputs.
