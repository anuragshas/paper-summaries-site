Joint Optimization of Relevance and Engagement in Multi-Task Ranking for E-Commerce with Efficient LLM Supervision

Authors: Luming Chen, Jiaqi Xi, Raghav Saboo, Kenny Chi, Martin Wang, Sudeep Das, Danny Nightingale, Aditya Dodda, Elyse Winer, Akshad Viswanathan

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2605.27704

Generated summary artifact for 2605.27704

Thesis

This paper proposes a production-scale multi-task ranking system that effectively integrates efficient LLM-based ordinal semantic relevance supervision as a first-class optimization objective, enabling explicit and controllable trade-offs between relevance and engagement without incurring online LLM inference overhead, thereby improving search quality and user trust in e-commerce platforms.

Core Problem

Optimizing industrial search ranking models solely for user engagement signals (e.g., click-through rate, add-to-cart rate, conversion rate) often introduces systematic biases, failing to satisfy semantic intent. A central challenge is the scarcity of high-quality semantic relevance supervision at production scale.

Key Novelty

A production-scale multi-task ranking framework that integrates efficient LLM-based ordinal semantic relevance supervision as a first-class optimization objective, enabling explicit and controllable relevance–engagement trade-offs without incurring online LLM inference overhead.

Key Contributions

  • The proposed ordinal formulation achieves substantially higher relevance NDCG@10 (0.962) compared to an engagement-only baseline (0.812), showing a +0.150 delta.
  • It maintains comparable performance on engagement metrics (AUC and NDCG@10 for Click, Add-to-cart, Conversion) relative to the engagement-only baseline, demonstrating no compromise of core behavioral objectives.
  • Online A/B experiments show statistically significant improvements in key business metrics: +1.16% ATCR, +1.10% CVR, and +0.50% Gross Order Value (GOV).
  • The ordinal relevance formulation consistently yields a more favorable trade-off frontier between relevance NDCG@10 and conversion NDCG@10 compared to softmax and regression baselines.
  • The fine-tuned LLM achieves 89% three-class accuracy and 98% within-1 accuracy in generating relevance labels compared to human annotations on a hold-out set of 105k query-item pairs.

Results

  • Integrating LLM-based relevance supervision into a multi-task ranking framework significantly improves semantic relevance without compromising core engagement metrics.
  • The proposed ordinal relevance formulation consistently outperforms other relevance-aware baselines (softmax, regression) in terms of the relevance vs. conversion trade-off.
  • Directly incorporating ordinal semantic relevance into the unified value function yields substantial relevance gains and positive online business impact, unlike treating it merely as an auxiliary task.

Limitations

  • Future work could explore richer semantic signals as inputs (e.g., query intent decomposition, attribute-level relevance).
  • The framework could be extended to replace global value model weights with query-adaptive weights.
  • The current approach can be extended to incorporate long-term user satisfaction metrics.
