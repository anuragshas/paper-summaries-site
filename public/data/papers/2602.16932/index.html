RankEvolve: Automating the Discovery of Retrieval Algorithms via LLM-Driven Evolution

Authors: Jinming Nian, Fangchen Li, Dae Hoon Park, Yi Fang

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16932

Generated summary artifact for 2602.16932

Thesis

Large language models, guided by an evaluator and evolutionary search, can automatically discover novel and improved lexical retrieval algorithms that outperform existing methods and generalize effectively to unseen datasets.

Core Problem

The development of effective lexical retrieval algorithms has historically relied on human expertise and iterative, intuition-driven parameter tuning, which limits the exploration of novel and potentially more effective scoring functions.

Key Novelty

RankEvolve introduces the first LLM-guided program evolution framework for automatically discovering novel and effective lexical retrieval algorithms from scratch, rather than merely tuning existing ones.

Key Contributions

  • RankEvolve-evolved algorithms (BM25* and QL-Dir*) consistently and significantly outperform their human-designed seed counterparts and other baselines across various held-out IR benchmarks (BEIR, BRIGHT, TREC DL 19/20).
  • The evolved algorithms demonstrate strong generalization capabilities, showing improved performance on datasets not seen during the evolutionary training process.
  • The degree of structural freedom afforded to the LLM (e.g., 'freeform' seed design) is crucial for discovering highly effective and generalizable algorithms, outperforming constrained or composable approaches.
  • While effective, evolved algorithms are often more complex and incur higher query latency (up to 11x slower for BM25* Freeform) compared to the seeds, highlighting a trade-off between effectiveness and efficiency not explicitly optimized for.

Results

  • RankEvolve successfully discovers novel lexical retrieval algorithms that surpass traditional methods, demonstrating the efficacy of LLM-guided evolution for Information Retrieval.
  • The discovered algorithms show strong generalization capabilities to unseen datasets, indicating robustness beyond the training environment.
  • The degree of structural freedom given to the LLM during evolution directly correlates with the ceiling of achievable performance, with 'freeform' seeds yielding the best results by allowing broader architectural changes.
  • While the evolved algorithms are more effective, they are often more complex and incur higher latency, highlighting the need to include efficiency as an explicit optimization objective in future work.

Limitations

  • The evolved algorithms, while more effective, can be significantly more complex and incur substantially higher query latency compared to their simpler, human-designed seed counterparts, as efficiency was not an explicit optimization objective.
  • The current framework does not explicitly incorporate or incentivize algorithmic elegance, potentially leading to overly complex solutions when simpler ones might suffice for similar performance.
  • The effectiveness of the LLM-guided evolution is influenced by the structural freedom of the seed program, suggesting that initial design choices still play a critical role in the search space exploration.
