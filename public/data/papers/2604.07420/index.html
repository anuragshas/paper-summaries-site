Dual-Rerank: Fusing Sequential Dependencies and Utility for Generative Reranking

Authors: Chao Zhang, Shuai Lin, Chenglei Dai, Ye Qian, Mingyang Fan, Yi Zhang, Yi Wang, Jingwei Zhuo

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2604.07420

Generated summary artifact for 2604.07420

Thesis

Dual-Rerank is a unified framework for industrial reranking that bridges the structural gap between accurate autoregressive modeling and efficient non-autoregressive generation through sequential knowledge distillation, while stabilizing list-wise reinforcement learning for whole-page utility via a novel double-decoupling mechanism.

Core Problem

Industrial reranking faces a fundamental duality: the structural trade-off between the high accuracy of slow autoregressive models and the efficiency of inaccurate non-autoregressive models, compounded by an optimization gap where supervised learning suffers from exposure bias and reinforcement learning is unstable in high-throughput streaming environments.

Key Novelty

Dual-Rerank is the first unified framework to reconcile the accuracy-latency tension in industrial generative reranking by jointly updating an AR Teacher and a NAR Student through sequential distillation and stable, decoupled online reinforcement learning.

Key Contributions

  • Evaluated on a public benchmark (Avito) and a massive industrial dataset from Kuaishou with 1 billion interaction logs.
  • Dual-Rerank reduces average P99 latency by 43.7% compared to autoregressive production baselines.
  • Online A/B testing on 5% of production traffic showed a 1.107% increase in Long-View Rate and a 0.714% increase in Whole-Page CTR.
  • Demonstrated successful transfer of sequential dependency logic from an AR teacher to a NAR student, preventing Ranking Collapse.

Results

  • Dual-Rerank breaks the accuracy-latency trade-off by achieving higher AUC than the best AR baselines with significantly lower latency.
  • Sequential Knowledge Distillation is foundational; without it, NAR models suffer from structural Ranking Collapse.
  • The LDRO algorithm effectively stabilizes reinforcement learning against the massive reward shifts found in real-world traffic logs.
  • The Unimodal Concentration Hypothesis is empirically validated: reranking is far more deterministic than open-ended natural language generation.

Limitations

  • The framework relies on the Unimodal Concentration Hypothesis, which may not hold for highly ambiguous or non-hierarchical ranking tasks.
  • Inference-time reward scoring depends on the accuracy of the multi-task Reward Network ($R_{net}$).
