Thesis

By reframing the randomized treatment assignment in A/B-testing as a meta-policy and leveraging $\Delta$-Off-Policy Estimation, a novel experimental protocol can exploit policy overlap to obtain unbiased, variance-reduced estimates for Average Treatment Effects, thereby accelerating experimentation and enhancing statistical power.

Core Problem

Online controlled experiments (A/B-tests) are expensive and suffer from high variance, which limits statistical power in assessing treatment effects. Standard variance reduction techniques leverage model-based control variates but often remain agnostic to potential structural relationships between competing policies.

Key Novelty

Applying the $\Delta$-Off-Policy Estimation ($\Delta$-OPE) framework to online A/B-testing by interpreting treatment assignment as a meta-policy, which leverages policy overlap to accelerate experimentation and reduce variance.

Key Contributions

  • The policy-aware Delta-IPS estimator strictly dominates the standard Difference-in-Means (DiM) estimator in terms of Mean Squared Error (MSE) on Average Treatment Effects (ATE), demonstrating robustness across varying action space sizes and reducing MSE significantly in high-overlap regimes.
  • Optimal treatment allocation, deviating from a balanced 50/50 split, can yield an approximately 18% variance reduction compared to standard practice in asymmetric policy settings.
  • The Delta-MRDR objective significantly reduces ATE estimation variance by 75% compared to Delta-Beta-IPS and 8% compared to Delta-DR in heterogeneous environments, by focusing model capacity on regions of policy disagreement.
  • The Delta-Beta-perp-perp-DCG estimator effectively combats variance explosion in top-K ranking evaluation at high policy divergence, maintaining strict variance advantage over DiM and unbaselined Delta-DCG across the entire divergence spectrum.

Results

  • The proposed policy-aware $\Delta$-OPE estimators ($\Delta$-IPS, $\Delta$-DR) strictly dominate the standard Difference-in-Means (DiM) estimator, especially when policies exhibit common support (overlap).
  • Optimal treatment allocation can significantly deviate from the traditional 50/50 split, favoring exploratory policies to minimize estimation variance and maximize statistical power.
  • The $\Delta$-MRDR objective effectively reduces ATE estimation variance by concentrating model capacity on regions of policy disagreement.
  • The framework extends to combinatorial ranking action spaces via $\Delta$-DCG, which, when combined with position-specific optimal baselines, combats variance explosion at high policy divergence.

Limitations

  • Empirical validation is based solely on synthetic simulation environments, which, despite offering ground-truth counterfactuals, necessarily simplify reward structure, context distributions, and policy behavior, making real-world uplift predictions less certain.
  • The proposed estimators require knowledge of exact policy probabilities $\pi(a|x)$ and $\pi'(a|x)$, which can be computationally expensive or numerically unstable in large action spaces or sequence generation settings (e.g., for LLMs).
  • The variance-optimal traffic allocation $p^{\star}$ derivation assumes stationarity of the context distribution and homoskedasticity of outcomes for the analytical solution under $\Delta$-IPS, though an adaptive two-stage design is suggested to mitigate the latter.
  • The robustness of $p^{\star}$ to distribution shifts (common in live recommender systems) remains an open question, requiring future work on fully sequential adaptive designs.
