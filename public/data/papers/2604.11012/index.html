Min-k Sampling: Decoupling Truncation from Temperature Scaling via Relative Logit Dynamics

Authors: Yuanhao Ding, Meimingwei Li, Esteban Garces Arias, Matthias Aßenmacher, Christian Heumann, Chongsheng Zhang

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11012

Generated summary artifact for 2604.11012

Thesis

Min-k Sampling is a novel dynamic truncation strategy that analyzes the local shape of the sorted logit distribution to identify "semantic cliffs" and determine truncation boundaries, achieving strict temperature invariance and maintaining robust, high-quality performance across various tasks and temperatures, thereby overcoming limitations of existing probability-based and logit-space methods.

Core Problem

Existing LLM decoding sampling strategies (Top-k, Top-p, Min-p, Top-nσ) suffer from inherent limitations: extreme sensitivity to the temperature parameter (T) or susceptibility to long-tail noise, leading to performance degradation and "semantic collapse" at high temperatures.

Key Novelty

A novel dynamic truncation strategy for LLM decoding that identifies "semantic cliffs" in the local logit distribution via a position-weighted relative decay rate, achieving strict temperature invariance and robust performance across diverse generation tasks.

Key Contributions

  • Min-k consistently achieves higher reasoning accuracy (EM%) across diverse models and datasets, maintaining robust performance even at high temperatures (T=10.0) where traditional methods collapse to near-zero accuracy.
  • In creative writing, Min-k attains peak win rates in low-temperature settings (58.60% at T=1.0 on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct) and maintains strong performance at high temperatures (52.80% at T=10.0 on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct).
  • Human evaluation indicates a preference for Min-k over the Top-nσ baseline, with Min-k preferred in 37.5% of cases compared to 33.5% for Top-nσ.
  • Min-k demonstrates remarkable robustness to its core hyperparameter τ, with accuracy consistently lying within a narrow 74%-79% range across a broad hyperparameter space, significantly reducing reliance on extensive tuning.
  • Min-k effectively suppresses semantic noise, maintaining a negligible noise rate (<10%) even at extreme temperatures (T=10.0), contrasting with traditional methods that reach 100% noise saturation at T>=2.0.
  • Min-k introduces a marginal computational overhead (3.73% relative slowdown over greedy decoding), comparable to other advanced sampling strategies, making it suitable for real-time deployment without specialized kernels.

Results

  • Min-k consistently improves text quality and diversity, demonstrating superior stability across various temperature regimes (mid and high) in reasoning-centric tasks and creative writing.
  • Min-k exhibits strong resilience against high-temperature degradation, preventing the "semantic collapse" observed in traditional probability-based methods and preserving core reasoning capabilities.
  • Min-k is strictly temperature-invariant and shows low sensitivity to its core hyperparameter τ, making it robust, easy to deploy, and reducing reliance on extensive hyperparameter search.
  • The method effectively suppresses long-tail distributed noise, enabling models to maintain high generation quality and coherence while adapting to fluctuating uncertainty in token generation.

Limitations

  • Similar to Top-p and Min-p, Min-k relies on sorting or scanning the full logit distribution, which incurs a marginal computational overhead, though this is negligible compared to the model's forward pass.
  • The core assumption of Min-k—that a semantic boundary manifests as a distinct "cliff" in the logit curve—may be less effective in scenarios requiring the retrieval of extremely long-tail knowledge entities where the correct token possesses a very low probability indistinguishable from noise. Aggressive truncation in such cases might inadvertently filter out the correct answer.
