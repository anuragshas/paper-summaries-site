TextReg: Mitigating Prompt Distributional Overfitting via Regularized Text-Space Optimization

Authors: Lucheng Fu, Ye Yu, Yiyang Wang, Yiqiao Jin, Haibo Jin, B. Aditya Prakash, Haohan Wang

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2605.21318

Generated summary artifact for 2605.21318

Thesis

Prompt distributional overfitting, characterized by increasing prompt length (capacity cost) and narrow sample-specific rules (scope narrowness), can be effectively mitigated by a regularization framework, TextReg, that explicitly controls representational inefficiency during discrete text-space optimization, leading to improved out-of-distribution generalization for LLMs.

Core Problem

Existing prompt optimization methods for Large Language Models (LLMs) suffer from 'prompt distributional overfitting,' where iteratively refined prompts become overly long, accumulate narrow sample-specific rules, and generalize poorly beyond the training distribution (out-of-distribution generalization). This failure mode reflects a lack of representation control in discrete text-space optimization.

Key Novelty

TextReg is a regularization framework that mitigates prompt distributional overfitting in discrete text-space optimization by explicitly controlling 'representational inefficiency' (prompt length and rule narrowness) through a regularized textual-gradient approach.

Key Contributions

  • TextReg consistently outperforms existing prompt optimization methods (TextGrad, REVOLVE) on out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization across multiple reasoning benchmarks and LLM test engines.
  • Achieves accuracy gains of up to +11.8% over TextGrad and +16.5% over REVOLVE on challenging tasks like Tracking Shuffled Objects.
  • Preserves and extends the generalization benefits of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, unlike baselines that often degrade OOD accuracy below CoT's performance.
  • All three core components of TextReg (Dual-Evidence Gradient Purification, Semantic Edit Regularization, Regularization-Guided Prompt Update) are jointly necessary for its strong performance.
  • TextReg demonstrates robustness to individual LLM-driven component degradation, indicating its regularization signal is structural rather than capability-bound.

Results

  • TextReg consistently outperforms existing prompt optimization methods like TextGrad and REVOLVE in terms of out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization across diverse reasoning benchmarks and LLM test engines.
  • The method achieves substantial accuracy gains, particularly on more challenging task variants, with improvements up to +10.0% over TextGrad and +9.9% over REVOLVE on Tracking Shuffled Objects.
  • Unlike baseline methods which often degrade OOD accuracy below zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) performance, TextReg consistently preserves and extends the generalization benefits of CoT.
  • Ablation studies confirm that all three components of TextReg (Dual-Evidence Gradient Purification, Semantic Edit Regularization, and Regularization-Guided Prompt Update) are essential and jointly contribute to mitigating prompt distributional overfitting.
  • TextReg demonstrates robustness to using weaker LLM components within its optimization pipeline, suggesting its regularization signal captures structural aspects rather than being solely dependent on strong LLM capabilities for every module.

Limitations

  • TextReg currently targets single-turn reasoning tasks with well-defined behavioral rules.
  • Future work is needed to extend TextReg to open-ended generation, multi-turn prompts, and agent instructions, where rule structure may be less distinct and capacity costs might be shared across turns.
