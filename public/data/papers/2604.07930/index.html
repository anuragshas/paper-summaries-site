Unified Supervision for Walmart’s Sponsored Search Retrieval via Joint Semantic Relevance and Behavioral Engagement Modeling

Authors: Shasvat Desai, Md Omar Faruk Rokon, Jhalak Nilesh Acharya, Isha Shah, Hong Yao, Utkarsh Porwal, Kuang-chih Lee

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2604.07930

Generated summary artifact for 2604.07930

Thesis

We propose a unified supervision framework for bi-encoder retrieval in Walmart's sponsored search that makes semantic relevance the primary training signal, while incorporating multi-channel retrieval prior and user engagement as a preference signal among relevant items, ultimately outperforming the current production system in both offline evaluation and online A/B tests with consistent gains.

Core Problem

Modern search systems rely on user engagement signals (e.g., clicks) to supervise bi-encoder retrieval training at scale. However, engagement is an imperfect and noisy proxy for semantic relevance, often influenced by non-relevance factors like popularity, promotion, or price. Additionally, engagement data can be sparse, especially in contexts like sponsored search where ad slot availability and budgets limit impressions, hindering effective learning.

Key Novelty

A unified supervision framework for bi-encoder retrieval in e-commerce sponsored search, prioritizing semantic relevance as the primary training signal, while incorporating multi-channel retrieval priors for hard negative mining and user engagement as a preference signal only among relevant items.

Key Contributions

  • Achieved +7.8% gain in Average relevance@25, +10.5% in P@25, and +5.7% in NDCG@25 compared to the production system in offline evaluation.
  • Demonstrated statistically significant positive lifts across various online A/B test metrics, including Sponsored Ad Impressions (+0.60%), Add to Cart Rate (+0.99%), and Total Search Page Views per Session (+0.63%).
  • The engagement supervision component increases the share of highly engaged items in the Top 25 retrieved set (e.g., +14.3 pp at 50th percentile, +7.0 pp at 90th percentile) without sacrificing overall relevance.

Results

  • The proposed unified supervision framework significantly outperforms the production control system in offline retrieval quality metrics.
  • Adding the engagement signal *after* establishing relevance provides modest but consistent additional gains, helping to prioritize stronger candidates among already relevant items without degrading overall relevance.
  • Online A/B tests demonstrate statistically significant positive lifts across several business and ad engagement metrics, confirming real-world impact.
  • The model ensures that retrieved items are not only semantically relevant but also popular/highly engaged, better aligning with user preferences and downstream ranking objectives.

Limitations

  • The paper does not explicitly list limitations in a dedicated section; however, it frames the issues of engagement noisiness, sparsity, and exposure bias in prior methods as problems that its unified supervision framework is designed to overcome.
