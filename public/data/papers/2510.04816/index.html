On Predicting Post-Click Conversion Rate via Counterfactual Inference

Authors: Junhyung Ahn, Sanghack Lee

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2510.04816

Generated summary artifact for 2510.04816

Thesis

By leveraging counterfactual inference within a structural causal model to generate conversion labels for non-clicked samples, ESCIM can effectively mitigate sample selection bias and data sparsity, leading to more accurate CVR predictions in recommender systems across various real-world scenarios and latent conversion data.

Core Problem

Accurately predicting Conversion Rate (CVR) in recommender systems is challenging due to sample selection bias and data sparsity. Traditional CVR models are trained only on clicked samples, ignoring the vast number of non-clicked samples.

Key Novelty

A novel framework (ESCIM) for CVR prediction that generates counterfactual conversion labels for non-clicked samples via causal inference, addressing sample selection bias and data sparsity in recommender systems.

Key Contributions

  • ESCIM-max achieved the highest CVR AUC (0.6792) on Ali-CCP using MLP backbone, surpassing the state-of-the-art by +0.0049.
  • ESCIM-ratio achieved the highest CTCVR AUC on Ali-CCP across all backbones (except DeepFM), with an average improvement of +1.51%.
  • On AliExpress dataset, ESCIM-max achieved the highest CVR AUC across all countries, showing significant improvements of +1.03% (AE-NL) and +1.88% (AE-US) over baselines.
  • Online A/B tests showed ESCIM improved CVR by +17.35%, CTCVR by +5.60%, and reduced CPA by -23.36% on average over 5 days.
  • ESCIM-max outperformed baselines on latent conversion data, achieving CVR AUC of 0.6905 (+1.80%) and CTCVR AUC of 0.6289 (+3.66%), demonstrating better generalization.

Results

  • ESCIM consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across various backbones and datasets for both CVR and CTCVR prediction.
  • The superior performance is attributed to the more accurate generation of counterfactual conversion labels for non-clicked samples, reflecting true click behavior.
  • Online A/B tests validate the real-world effectiveness of ESCIM, showing significant improvements in CVR and CTCVR, and reduction in CPA.
  • Accurately estimating the posterior distribution of the exogenous variable Z is crucial for improved model performance.
  • ESCIM generalizes better to latent conversion samples, demonstrating robustness to sample selection bias and enabling more accurate predictions for users who didn't click during training but showed click behavior during testing.

Limitations

  • A more precise generation procedure for counterfactual labels could further enhance CVR prediction accuracy.
  • The process for inferring counterfactual CVR can be further refined, and more robust methods for converting counterfactual pCVRs into hard labels can be explored.
  • The effectiveness of ESCIM relies heavily on accurate CTR estimation, but achieving this remains a significant challenge, especially concerning the instability of inverse propensity weighting.
