TailSFT: Filtered Fine-Tuning Improves Post-Training Performance

Authors: Sadhika Malladi, Samy Jelassi, Dylan J. Foster, Jordan T. Ash, Akshay Krishnamurthy

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2608.25756

Generated summary artifact for 2608.25756

Thesis

By modifying supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to filter out already-fit sequences, TailSFT improves the coverage of reward-bearing responses, which in turn provides a better initialization for reinforcement learning (RL) and ultimately leads to enhanced post-training performance in language models for complex reasoning tasks.

Core Problem

Existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) pipelines, while optimizing for local objectives, often yield models that are suboptimal as initializations for subsequent reinforcement learning (RL), particularly by not adequately preserving or improving the coverage of useful, reward-bearing responses.

Key Novelty

A supervised fine-tuning (SFT) algorithm, TailSFT, which filters already-fit sequences based on their loss reduction relative to the initial policy, to improve coverage for subsequent reinforcement learning (RL).

Key Contributions

  • TailSFT significantly improves pass@16 (coverage) by up to 16.79% absolute on coding tasks and 3.07% on math tasks when fine-tuning OLMo-3 7B.
  • Higher-coverage SFT checkpoints produced by TailSFT consistently translate into better post-RL performance, showing up to 3.93% absolute pass@1 gains after GRPO.
  • TailSFT improves post-RL pass@1 in every matched comparison (100% of tested cases) compared to standard SFT initialization.
  • A lightweight coverage-ratio diagnostic is introduced, providing a sufficient condition to identify settings where TailSFT is most likely to improve coverage.

Results

  • TailSFT, a supervised fine-tuning method, significantly improves pass@16 (coverage) for OLMo-3 7B models across 15 out of 18 math and coding benchmarks.
  • The higher-coverage SFT checkpoints generated by TailSFT consistently lead to improved pass@1 performance after subsequent GRPO, with absolute gains up to 3.93%.
  • TailSFT's benefits are more pronounced for large K coverage during SFT, but consistently translate to better single-sample accuracy post-RL.
  • A proposed coverage-ratio diagnostic helps identify scenarios where TailSFT is most likely to preserve or improve coverage.

Limitations

  • In controlled graph navigation experiments, TailSFT was slightly less effective than other filtering variants when prompts were homogeneous, suggesting that the initial policy reference might not always provide useful signal in simpler, uniform settings.
  • The coverage-ratio diagnostic ($\\rho_{16}$) provides a sufficient but not necessary condition for TailSFT to improve coverage, meaning some cases with $\\rho_{16} < 1$ can still benefit, indicating room for more precise diagnostic tools.
