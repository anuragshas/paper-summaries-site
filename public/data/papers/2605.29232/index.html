On the Practice of Scaling Search Conversion Rate Prediction

Authors: James Pak Jyun-Yu Jiang Fan Zhang Sen Wang Taekmin Kim Henry Tsai Vijay Rajaram Juexin Lin Mohitdeep Singh Alessandro Magnani Johnny Chen Qian Zhao Rao Fu Zhirong Liang Jordan Gilliland Winter Jiao Coupang

Thesis

This paper presents an effective and general mechanism for scaling modern search CVR prediction models by empirically studying the scaling performance of state-of-the-art architectures, identifying key scaling factors (backbone, embedding, data), and demonstrating robust inference optimizations to balance model quality with production constraints like training cost and serving latency.

Core Problem

Scaling a Search Conversion Rate (CVR) prediction model in high-traffic environments is challenging due to the need to balance superior model quality with strict constraints on training cost and serving latency.

Key Novelty

A comprehensive empirical study and practical framework for scaling modern search CVR prediction models, identifying key scaling factors, demonstrating their additive effects, and proposing effective training and inference optimization strategies for real-world deployment.

Key Contributions

  • Achieved a combined +2.1% offline mAP improvement and a +2.6% lift in online search conversion rate through A/B testing, compared to a pre-scaling baseline.
  • Deployed models with an 8x increase in inference compute (FLOPs) while maintaining minimal latency impact due to dedicated inference optimizations.
  • A streamlined warmstart strategy reduced training time overhead to just +36%, significantly improving iteration frequency and velocity of model updates compared to a +264% cold-start approach.

Results

  • Selecting the right model backbone and scaling factors is crucial for performance and efficiency, with MaskNet often outperforming sequence models within the same FLOPs budget.
  • The impact of backbone, embedding, and data scaling on model quality is largely independent and additive, allowing for separate optimization.
  • Embedding dimension scaling is more effective than vocabulary size scaling for item embeddings; for data scaling, longer training durations yield log-linear mAP gains with diminishing returns.
  • Streamlined warmstart strategies significantly accelerate training iterations and reduce overhead, maintaining model quality.
  • Inference optimization techniques, such as hybrid CPU-GPU execution and dynamic batching, are essential to meet low-latency GPU serving requirements for high-capacity models.
  • Combined scaling strategies and infrastructure optimizations led to substantial online gains in search conversion rate (+2.6%) in real-world A/B tests.

Limitations

  • Scaling approaches may not work uniformly across all cases, requiring exhaustive exploration to identify the optimal scaling recipe.
  • While DHEN-based ensembles consistently outperform individual models, optimized single-model backbones can achieve better Pareto-optimality in the long term under the same FLOPs budget.
  • Scaling text embeddings for product titles did not provide performance gains, hypothesized to be due to the general nature of text tokens compared to specific categorical features.
  • Simple volume scaling of training data shows diminishing returns and can be cost-ineffective without strategies like warmstarting.
