Black-Box On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models

Authors: Tianzhu Ye, Li Dong, Zewen Chi, Xun Wu, Shaohan Huang, Furu Wei

Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10643

Generated summary artifact for 2511.10643

Thesis

Generative Adversarial Distillation (GAD), a framework that frames the student LLM as a generator and trains a discriminator to distinguish its responses from the teacher, enables effective on-policy and black-box distillation of large language models, consistently outperforming sequence-level knowledge distillation and achieving strong generalization.

Core Problem

Black-box distillation trains a student model using only the textual outputs of a teacher, without access to its internal logits or parameters. The absence of fine-grained probability supervision makes conventional likelihood-based objectives unavailable, and on-policy learning without explicit feedback on the quality of student generations is infeasible.

Key Novelty

Introducing Generative Adversarial Distillation (GAD), a novel framework for black-box LLM distillation that enables on-policy learning without access to teacher logits or internal states by employing a generator-discriminator minimax game.

Key Contributions

  • GAD consistently surpasses SeqKD and instruction models without distillation across various LLM sizes and datasets (GPT-4o scores).
  • Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct distilled with GAD achieves performance comparable to the proprietary GPT-5-Chat teacher on the LMSYS-Chat benchmark.
  • GAD demonstrates strong improvements in out-of-distribution generalization, where SeqKD often performs poorly, sometimes yielding negative gains.
  • Human evaluations validate GAD's superiority with high win rates (exceeding 50%) and low loss rates (below 30%) against baselines.
  • Ablation studies confirm the importance of warmup strategies, Bradley-Terry loss, and balanced generator-discriminator sizes for optimal performance, and GAD's resistance to reward hacking.

Results

  • GAD consistently outperforms the instruct model before distillation and the SeqKD baseline across all datasets and model variants in automatic evaluation (GPT-4o scores).
  • GAD shows particularly strong gains in out-of-distribution generalization compared to SeqKD, which often yields marginal or negative improvements.
  • On GPT-4o score, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct distilled with GAD approaches the capability of the GPT-5 teacher itself.
  • Human evaluations confirm GAD's superiority, achieving win rates exceeding 50% and loss rates below 30% against baselines.
  • SeqKD tends to overfit to local patterns, while GAD captures global stylistic characteristics and learns reachable modes, leading to better generalization.
  • The on-policy discriminator in GAD is stable and robust against reward hacking over thousands of training steps, unlike off-policy discriminators.
