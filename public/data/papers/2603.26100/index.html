Rethinking Recommendation Paradigms: From Pipelines to Agentic Recommender Systems

Authors: Jinxin Hu, Hao Deng, Lingyu Mu, Hao Zhang, Shizhun Wang, Yu Zhang, Xiaoyi Zeng

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2603.26100

Thesis

This paper proposes Agentic Recommender Systems (AgenticRS) as a conceptual framework and design principles to move from static, manually tuned architectures to self-evolving ecosystems for large-scale industrial recommender systems.

Core Problem

Large-scale industrial recommender systems currently rely on fixed multi-stage pipelines and 'One Model' designs, which are static, black boxes, and challenging to scale due to heterogeneous data, multi-objective constraints, and high manual iteration costs.

Key Novelty

The paper introduces Agentic Recommender Systems (AgenticRS), a paradigm shift from static pipelines to self-evolving multi-agent decision systems, where key modules are reframed as autonomous agents capable of independent evolution.

Key Contributions

  • The paper introduces a novel conceptual framework for Agentic Recommender Systems, proposing a paradigm shift towards self-evolving, multi-agent architectures.
  • It clearly articulates the limitations of current static recommender pipelines in handling heterogeneity, multi-objective optimization, and autonomous improvement.
  • The framework details how individual modules can be reframed as agents with functional closed loops, independent evaluability, and evolvable decision spaces.
  • It outlines specific evolution mechanisms, including RL- and LLM-based approaches, for both individual agent refinement and compositional system restructuring.
  • The proposed layered architecture (Decision, Evolution, Infrastructure) and hierarchical reward design offer a structured approach to managing complexity and aligning local agent objectives with global system goals.

Results

  • N/A (No experimental results are presented as this is a conceptual paper).

Limitations

  • The paper is a conceptual framework and does not present empirical results or a specific algorithm, but rather a blueprint for future development.
  • Implementing and evaluating such an agentic system in a real-world industrial setting poses significant engineering challenges.
