Retail Product Search: A Practical Approach at Target

Authors: Darshan Sonagara, Qujiaheng Zhang, Ankit Singh, Alex Li

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/arXiv:2609.31498

Generated summary artifact for 2609.31498

Thesis

A hybrid search system combining lexical and semantic retrieval, optimized with engagement-derived labels, precision control, and efficient deployment, can significantly improve user engagement and conversion in retail product search at scale compared to lexical-only approaches.

Core Problem

Retail product search systems face challenges in balancing relevance, revenue, and profit, handling diverse user intents (exact matches to open-ended discovery), and maintaining low latency at scale. Traditional keyword-based methods often fail with natural language queries, and pure vector search can yield low-precision results or miss exact matches.

Key Novelty

A robust and scalable hybrid lexical-semantic retrieval system for retail product search, featuring novel engagement-derived hard negative mining, precision control, and weighted interleaving for result fusion, demonstrated with significant online business impact.

Key Contributions

  • Achieved a +0.97% lift in Click-Through Rate (CTR) over the lexical-only baseline in online A/B tests.
  • Showed a +0.98% lift in order conversion rate and a +1.10% lift in demand per visitor in online A/B tests.
  • Reduced zero-result searches by approximately 50% relative to the lexical-only baseline.
  • Demonstrated significant improvements in offline retrieval quality metrics (NDCG, MRR) through domain adaptation, stratified sampling, and hard negative mining.
  • Precision control filters significantly improved the precision of vector retrieval by removing off-target items.
  • Successfully deployed the system in production, meeting a p99 latency target below 50 ms on CPU-based infrastructure.

Results

  • The hybrid lexical and semantic retrieval system significantly outperformed a lexical-only baseline on engagement and conversion metrics in live A/B tests.
  • Weighted interleaving proved more effective than Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) for merging results when channels have little overlap, which is common in product search.
  • Domain adaptation, achieved through large engagement-derived datasets, stratified sampling, and hard negative mining, was crucial for performance, outweighing gains from larger pretrained models.
  • A precision control module is vital to filter potentially irrelevant vector search results based on explicit and implicit query attributes, ensuring high-quality and trusted results.
  • The system achieved high scalability and low latency targets (p99 below 50 ms) on CPU-based infrastructure, demonstrating practical deployability for millions of daily queries.

Limitations

  • The current system returns the same candidates to every user, lacking personalization. Future work could integrate user-level signals (browsing, purchase history) to condition query embeddings, balancing personalization with diversity to avoid limiting product discovery.
  • The system primarily relies on text. Future work includes incorporating multimodal (image) embeddings and multi-channel retrieval (behavioral co-occurrence, structured attributes) to capture richer product information and broaden recall.
  • The precision control module uses a fixed set of NER-based and classifier-based filters with offline-tuned thresholds. A limitation is the lack of adaptability to query at inference time; a learned model could provide soft scores to handle ambiguous or multi-intent queries more effectively.
